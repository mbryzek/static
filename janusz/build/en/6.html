6. APPLICATION OF THE NEW COMPENSATION METHOD IN A PIEZORESISTIVE PRESSURE TRANSDUCER

s. 79

6.1. Introduction

The method proposed by the author arose in the course of the development of a new pneumatic-to-electric pressure transducer at the Mera-Pnefal Industrial Automation Research and Development Centre (OBRAP). In the previously manufactured inter-system converter type A271 [33] a burdensome and laborious compensation procedure was used, requiring the winding of long-term stable resistors in the range 0.1 to 5 Ω with an accuracy of the order of 10 %. Moreover, the compensation method initially applied in that transducer infringed the patent claims of West European firms, practically preventing the export of the transducers.1

Soon after the author had developed the new method, it was tested in the pneumatic-to-electric pressure transducer type A272, and then, after positive results of the prototype tests, by decision of the Technical Council it was introduced into series production at Mera-Pnefal. In 1978 the manufacture of a series of 1000 units of these transducers is planned.

In the remainder of this work there will be presented the required parameters of the transducer, the construction and properties of the pressure sensor, a design outline of the more interesting subassemblies of the transducer connected with the temperature compensation circuit, and finally some results of the tests of the prototypes of the pneumatic-to-electric transducer carried out by the Product Testing Laboratory of the Quality Control Department of the Mera-Pnefal Industrial Automation Enterprise and by the Mera-Pnefal Industrial Automation Research and Development Centre.

6.3. Sensor

6.3.1. Construction

The pressure sensor is built on the basis of silicon strain gauges. The input pressure is converted by means of the bellows assembly (Fig. 27) into a force producing stresses in the strain-gauge beam. The beam is rigidly fixed at one end, while at the other it is coupled to the bellows assembly. Two silicon strain gauges are bonded to the strain-gauge beam, one on each side. Under the action of the input pressure this beam deflects, which causes compressive stresses to arise in the upper gauge TS2 and tensile stresses in the lower gauge TS1, as a result of which an increase in the resistance value (of approx. 5 %) of gauge TS1 and a decrease in the resistance value of gauge TS2 are obtained. The beam is protected from overload by the beam travel stop. The gauge leads are soldered to the terminal board, from which the sensor signal is then conveyed by wires to the electronic

signal-conditioning circuit.

The basic technical data of the sensor are: a. initial resistance of the gauges at a temperature of 293 K, at atmospheric pressure - 110 Ω ±11 Ω, b. spread of resistance of the gauges on one sensing beam 0 to 5 Ω, the gauge in tension having the greater resistance, c. full-scale resistance change of the gauges (for a change of pressure from 20 kPa to 100 kPa) - 5.6 Ω to 7.6 Ω.

6.3.2. Sources of temperature errors.

The basic source of the temperature errors of the sensor is the strain gauges. Both the initial resistance and the sensitivity of the gauges change strongly with temperature. The change of the initial resistance at atmospheric pressure (without stress) as a function of temperature for the gauges used has the character of a second-order curve. The sensitivity is determined by the relation:

An additional influence on the drift of the lower range value is exerted by the following parameters:

  • differences between the linear coefficients of thermal expansion of the gauge, the adhesive, the insulating dielectric layer and the beam material,
  • differences between the temperature coefficients of the bracket and the bellows assembly, and of the beam and the base,
  • the sensitivity drift, if the lower range value occurs with the beam under preload. The sensitivity drift depends moreover on the following factors that vary as a function of temperature:
  • the effective area of the bellows and its compliance
  • the Young's modulus of the strain-gauge beam
  • the thickness of the layers insulating the gauges from the beam.

From the above data it follows that the linear approximation of the temperature drift of the span is a very good approximation of the real error, and almost all transducers should have this error smaller than 0.1 %/10 K as a result of applying the proposed compensation procedure. The temperature drift of the zero lends itself somewhat less well (by two accuracy classes) to compensation by a compensating signal linear in temperature; nevertheless the majority of transducers (99.3 %) will probably have an uncompensated zero drift smaller than 0.25 %/10 K.

6.4. Electronic circuit of the transducer

6.4.1. Block diagram

The block diagram of the transducer circuit design chosen by the author [87] is shown in Fig. 31. The transducer is built of six main assemblies

  • the measuring bridge MP
  • the single bridge MK1MK_{1}MK1​ for the temperature compensation of the lower range value
  • the voltage-difference-to-current converter W
  • the current source I
  • the single bridge MK2MK_{2}MK2​ for the temperature compensation of the upper range value (span)
  • the regulated power supply

The measuring bridge makes it possible to convert the change of resistance of the strain gauges TS into a change of voltage on the bridge diagonal. At the same time, as a result of the differential connection of the gauges, a preliminary compensation of the effect of temperature is realized. In addition the circuit makes it possible to null the voltage on the bridge diagonal at the lower range value and

s. 89

to add to the voltage signal from the gauges the voltage of the generator of the zero temperature-compensation signal, in the form of the single bridge MK1MK_{1}MK1​.

The single bridge MK1MK_{1}MK1​ makes it possible to null the measuring bridge at the reference temperature independently of the setting of potentiometer P4P_{4}P4​, because on this potentiometer, connected across the diagonal of the compensation bridge MK1MK_{1}MK1​, there is zero voltage at the reference temperature. At the compensation temperature a voltage is produced on potentiometer P4P_{4}P4​, a part of which, set by the position of the potentiometer wiper, is used to null the measuring bridge again, i.e. to compensate the temperature drift of the lower range value.

The measuring bridge is supplied by the current source I. The source current of this source is controlled by the potential of the wiper of potentiometer P1P_{1}P1​, placed on the diagonal of the single bridge MK2MK_{2}MK2​. At the reference temperature there is zero voltage on this potentiometer and the source current is determined by the value of the potential of the end terminals of the potentiometer, independently of the position of its wiper. At the first compensation temperature a certain voltage is produced on the potentiometer, and consequently a change in the position of the potentiometer wiper changes the value of the current supplying the measuring bridge, which is used to compensate the temperature error of the upper range value.

The differential signal from the diagonal of the measuring bridge MP is converted by the circuit W, with an adjustable conversion factor, into the output current I1I_{1}I1​. To produce the voltage output signal of the transducer there serves a resistor R shunting the output terminals.

The voltage regulator S supplies two supply voltages:

  • the internal supply reference voltage +19 V
  • the auxiliary voltage for supplying the transducer circuit +21 V

The whole circuit is supplied from a single voltage with a nominal value of 25 V.

6.4.2. Choice of the temperature-sensing elements

The following components were considered as temperature-sensing elements:

  • diodes
  • transistors
  • metal resistors
  • thermistors

Diodes and transistors are characterized both by a large temperature coefficient of the forward or base-emitter voltage (approx. 3 %/10 K) and by a highly linear change of this voltage over a wide temperature range. Among the basic drawbacks of these components must be counted the large spread of the value of this voltage when discrete components are used, of the order of the full-scale signal of the sensor. Moreover, their use would increase the common-mode voltage of the sensor by several hundred

s. 91

millivolts. An additional argument that influenced the rejection of these components is the fact that if, in the state of bridge balance, the supply current were to change, this would cause bridge unbalance on account of the stabilizing characteristics of these components and of the voltage on the resistive components being proportional to the current.

Metal resistors, it is true, are also characterized by a linear temperature coefficient, but the maximum temperature coefficient (approx. 2 %/10 K for copper) requires the use of large values of resistance in the compensation bridge, giving in effect a large common-mode voltage. The voltage of the bridge dividers moreover requires additional linearization. An additional drawback is the absence of series production of miniature components of this type.

Thermistors are characterized by the highest temperature coefficient (of the order of 40 to 110 %/10 K), by the possibility of choosing the value from the standard value series of series-produced miniature components, whereas among their drawbacks must be counted the large nonlinearity of the temperature characteristic. Since this drawback can be eliminated by a suitable linearizing circuit, thermistors were chosen for use in the transducer. In the remainder of this work the circuits linearizing the characteristics of the thermistors are presented.

6.4.3. Optimization of the characteristics of the bridge compensating the zero drift

The circuit of the bridge compensating the temperature drift of the zero should provide a linear change of voltage with changes of temperature, adjustable in the range from -8 %/80 K to +8 %/80 K. It was decided to check the circuit of a single-thermistor voltage divider with regard to the possibility of obtaining a voltage on the bridge arms that is suitably linear as a function of temperature (Fig. 32). The bridge is built of two such dividers connected in opposition. As a result the voltage UABU_{AB}UAB​ decreases, whereas the voltage UCBU_{CB}UCB​ increases with temperature.

In order to reduce the effect of changes of the bridge resistance on the voltage UDBU_{DB}UDB​ under constant-current excitation, a shunt resistor R was introduced.

For the linearization of the voltages UABU_{AB}UAB​ and UCBU_{CB}UCB​ as a function of temperature, use was made of the nonlinear relation determining the value of the resultant resistance of the resistor R2R_{2}R2​ and the thermistor RT connected in parallel, and of the nonlinear relation determining the division ratio of the resistive divider R1R_{1}R1​ - R2R_{2}R2​/RT.

The optimum values of the resistors R1R_{1}R1​, R2R_{2}R2​ were calculated by means of a computer program. The calculations were carried out for 8 pairs of temperatures (reference temperature ToT_{o}To​ - compensation temperature T1T_{1}T1​), starting from 288 K - 298 K and ending at 274 K - 312 K, where at each successive step both temperatures were changed simultaneously by 2 K. The optimization consisted in minimizing the slope of the nonlinearity of the divider with respect to the temperature 293 K.

6.4.4. Optimization of the characteristics of the bridge compensating the span drift

The maximum range of variation of the signal correcting the span drift should be 2.5 times greater than in the case of the zero drift. In connection with this it may be assumed that the nonlinearity and the slope of the nonlinearity function of the divider circuit used in the zero compensation bridge would increase in the same ratio. Since the span drift being corrected does not change sign (-0.5 %/10 K to -2.5 %/10 K), it is enough to place the thermistors in one arm of the bridge. 6.6.3. Test results

On the basis of the tests it was found, among other things, that the maximum intrinsic error of the prototypes does not exceed 0.25 %, a change of the supply voltage in the interval 23 to 31 V gives an additional error smaller than 0.04 %, whereas a change of the load resistance causes an additional error smaller than 0.01 %.12

The temperature error was determined in the test report [89] with the intrinsic error included. As the temperature error of the transducer was taken the greatest slope of the changes of the transducer output signal at 20 kPa or 100 kPa with respect to the temperature 293 K. With this interpretation the following maximum temperature errors were obtained:

  • for the transducer with the 0 to 5 mA range - 0.193 %/10 K
  • for the transducer with the 0 to 20 mA range - 0.128 %/10 K
  • for the transducer with the 4 to 20 mA range - 0.050 %/10 K

In the tests carried out at OBR AP Mera-Pnefal, in determining the temperature error the intrinsic error was disregarded, and only the change of the output signal for the zero (20 kPa) and for the span (80 kPa) caused by the change of temperature was taken into account. It amounts to

  • for the transducer with the 0 to 20 mA range - limiting zero drift 0.07 %/10 K, limiting span drift 0.11 %/10 K
  • for the transducer with the 4 to 20 mA range - limiting zero drift 0.06 %/10 K, limiting span drift 0.09 %/10 K

s. 106

As may be observed, the temperature drift of both the zero and the span can be coarsely approximated by a quadratic curve, with the exception of the zero drift in the transducer with the 4 to 20 mA output signal range, where the drift can be approximated by a straight line. The parabolic course of the temperature error is obvious once it is taken into account that the transducers were compensated with a compensating signal linear in temperature, so that the higher-order components were left uncompensated. The zero drift of the transducer with the 4 to 20 mA output signal range suggests an incorrectly performed compensation procedure. Taking the measurement error into account, however, it can be stated that a more accurate performance of the compensation was not possible.
