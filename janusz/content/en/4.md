4. A NEW METHOD OF COMPENSATION OF TEMPERATURE ERRORS

4.1. Introductory remarks

The new method of compensation of temperature errors proposed below [81, 82] eliminates the drawbacks of the known methods, while at the same time making it possible to reduce the temperature error of the transducer identically to zero.

In order to make the considerations presented below unambiguous, restrictions will be stated for the class of transducers whose compensation will be analysed:
(a) the subject of the considerations will be transducers with a linear transfer characteristic from pressure to an electrical signal,
(b) it is assumed that the only source of the temperature errors of the transducer is the sensor. If it is required to take into account the temperature drifts of other units of the transducer located at the same temperature as the sensor (e.g. the electronic signal-conditioning circuit), they should be referred to the level of the sensor, and the equivalent sensor error (in the sense of Section 2 of this thesis) should be subject to compensation,
(c) additivity of the value of the measured parameter and of the value of the temperature error is assumed, i.e. it is assumed that the value of the parameter under consideration at an arbitrary temperature is described by the sum of the value of the parameter at the reference temperature and the temperature error,
(d) the subject of the considerations will be errors in temperature steady states; the effect of temperature in the transient state will not be analysed, because this error is inseparably linked with the design of the transducer.

4.2. Sufficient conditions for the temperature compensation of the transfer characteristic of the transducer

Let the transfer characteristic (2) of the transducer be described by a linear relation (Fig. 14):

$$ U = U_{p} + \alpha_{1} X \qquad (3) $$

where:
   \(U_{p}\) - the lower range value of the transducer output signal, at the lower range value \(X_{p}\) of the non-electrical quantity
   \(\alpha_{1}\) - the sensitivity

[TN: from here on \(U_{p}\) denotes the output at the lower range value; in Section 3.2 the same symbol denoted the forward voltage of a p-n junction.]

The changes of the output signal under the effect of temperature are determined by the partial differential equation

$$ \frac{\partial U}{\partial T} = \frac{\partial U_{p}}{\partial T} + X \frac{\partial \alpha_{1}}{\partial T} \qquad (4) $$

In order to satisfy the condition of complete temperature compensation of the output signal

$$ \frac{\partial U}{\partial T} = 0 \qquad (5) $$

two solutions of equation (4) are obtained

a.
$$ \frac{\partial U_{p}}{\partial T} = - X \frac{\partial \alpha_{1}}{\partial T} \qquad (6) $$

b.
$$ \left\{ \begin{aligned} \frac{\partial U_{p}}{\partial T} &= 0 \\ \frac{\partial \alpha_{1}}{\partial T} &= 0 \end{aligned} \right. \qquad (7) $$

Relation (17) therefore expresses the condition of additivity of the value of the measured parameter and of the temperature error, formulated in Section 4.1 (c).

4.3. The essence of complete temperature compensation

4.3.1. Synthesis of the method

Making use of the possibility, described above, of temperature compensation of the transfer characteristic by compensation of the lower and upper range values of the measuring range, we shall present a new method of compensation of each of these parameters.

Let us define the absolute temperature error of the sensor as the difference between the value of the sensor output signal at the pressure \(X\) under consideration at a given temperature \(T\) and at the reference temperature \(T_{o}\), denoting it by \(\delta(T)\):

$$ \delta(T) = f(X, T) - f(X, T_{o}) \qquad (18) $$

Let us denote the maximum absolute value of the error 18 by \(\delta_{m}\)

$$ \delta_{m} = \left| \delta(T) \right|_{\max} \qquad (19) $$

In an analogous way we shall define the absolute temperature error of the transducer \(\Delta(T)\)

$$ \Delta(T) = g(X, T) - g(X, T_{o}) \qquad (20) $$

and the maximum absolute value of the error (20) by \(\Delta_{m}\)

$$ \Delta_{m} = \left| \Delta(T) \right|_{\max} \qquad (21) $$

If we denote by \(U_{p}\) and \(U_{k}\) respectively the values of the sensor output signal corresponding to the lower and upper range values of the measured pressure \(X\), then the effect of the action of the compensation circuit will be a reduction of the maximum relative measurement error of the transducer in comparison with the maximum relative error of the sensor (Fig. 16):

$$ \frac{\delta_{m}}{\left| U_{k} - U_{p} \right|} > \frac{\Delta_{m}}{\left| U_{k} - U_{p} \right|} \qquad (22) $$

4.3.3. Sufficient conditions for complete temperature compensation

     The condition of zero temperature error of the transducer at n compensation temperatures is not sufficient to determine its maximum value. For this, knowledge of the value of the error over the whole operating temperature range under consideration is necessary. We shall now consider the sufficient conditions for complete temperature compensation in the sense of minimization of the value of the temperature error.
     Let us introduce the following description of the temperature error of the sensor \(\delta(T)\)

$$ \delta(T) = c_{o} + c_{1} T + c ... + c_{s} \; T^{s} \qquad (35) $$

where \(c_{o}\),..., \(c_{s}\)  - constants

By virtue of the Weierstrass approximation theorem [83] (see Section 4.2), expression (35) approximates any continuous curve sufficiently accurately, the degree s of the approximating polynomial being a function of the accuracy of approximation. Let us therefore assume that the description (35) of the temperature error of the sensor is a sufficiently accurate representation of the real error (18) of the sensor.
     Comparing expressions (29) and (23) we may determine the compensating signal as follows


<!-- scan p056 | printed page 53 | type: equations -->

$$ H(T) = w_{o} + \sum_{i=1}^{n} w_{i} \cdot \prod_{j=0}^{i-1} a_{j} \cdot (T - T_{j}) \qquad (36) $$

Expression (36) is a polynomial of degree n.
     Thus the temperature error (23) of the transducer is the sum of two polynomials (35), (36), modified by the introduction of the coefficients \(b_{1}\), \(b_{2}\) of the normalizing circuit.
The minimum value that the sum of two polynomials can take is zero. If two polynomials are of the same degree and the coefficients of the variable of the same degree likewise have equal absolute values and opposite signs, then a zero value of the sum of these polynomials is obtained irrespective of the value of the variable.
     We may therefore formulate the conclusion that the optimum condition of temperature compensation that interests us is the attainment of the identity

$$ b_{1} \cdot \delta(T) + b_{2} \cdot H(T) \equiv 0 \qquad (37) $$

A necessary condition for attaining (37) is the equality of the degrees of the polynomials (35) and (36):

$$ n = s \qquad (38) $$

which is equivalent to equating the number n of compensation temperatures with the degree s of the polynomial approximating the temperature error of the sensor.
     As a result of carrying out the compensation in accordance with Section 4.3.2, one obtains (n+1) zeros of the equation:

$$ \Delta(T) = 0 $$

     Since through (n+1) points only one curve of degree n can be drawn, this means that the compensation procedure leads unambiguously to the identity (37). This is equivalent to the identical vanishing of the temperature error of the transducer over the whole operating temperature range, irrespective of the choice of the compensation temperatures.
     In the analysis carried out above it was assumed that the compensating-signal generators \(H_{1}\)...\(H_{n}\) produce signals linearly dependent on temperature. The resultant compensating signal is produced

<!-- scan p057 | printed page 54 | type: equations -->

by establishing an appropriate combination of products of these signals, giving as a result a polynomial of degree \(n\). Let us now assume that we wish to obtain a polynomial of degree \(n\) with a smaller number of compensation temperatures. To this end let us make the component functions \(f_{j}(T)\) dependent on temperature in the following way:

$$ f_{j}(T) = (T - T_{j}) \cdot \sum_{p=0}^{m-1} a_{jp} \cdot (T - T_{j})^{p} \qquad (39) $$

where \(a_{jp}\) - constants
      \(j = 0, 1, ..., n-1\)
      \(m\)   - a natural number

Expression (39) is a polynomial of degree \(m\) and, by virtue of the Weierstrass approximation theorem [83] (see Section 4.2), it can describe any continuous function, the degree \(m\) of the polynomial depending on the required accuracy of approximation of that function.
     The adopted form (39) of the component functions \(f_{j}(T)\) also has the property (28) of vanishing identically at the temperatures \(T_{j}\).
     Let us assume in turn that a certain number \(R\) of properties of relation (35) approximating the temperature error of the sensor is known in advance. Let the degree \(m\) of the component function (39) be determined as follows

$$ m = R + r \qquad (40) $$

where \(r\) is a natural number

By raising the degree \(m\) one obtains a compensating polynomial \(H(T)\) of degree \(n(R+r)\). In order to obtain the identity (37) the relation

$$ s = n(R + r) \qquad (41) $$

must be satisfied, whence one obtains the required number \(n\) of compensation temperatures in the case of adopting \(r = 0\), that is, in the case of maximum use of the \(R\) known properties:

$$ n = \frac{s}{R} \qquad (42) $$

55

Conclusions: the optimum conditions of complete temperature compensation can be achieved either by producing component functions \(f_j(T)\) linear as a function of temperature and carrying out the compensation at a number of temperatures equal to the degree of the polynomial describing the temperature error of the sensor (condition 38), or else by raising the degree of the polynomial describing the component function \(f_j(T)\), relying on properties of the temperature error of the sensor known in advance, and reducing the number of compensation temperatures (condition 42).

An example of advance information on the character of the temperature error of the sensor is information on the ratio of the values of the errors at certain temperatures. Thus in integrated pressure sensors [40] the temperature span drift is approximated by a fourth-order curve, and the quotient of the values of the temperature error at the temperatures 352 K and 241 K is a function of the substrate resistivity. In such a case it may prove sufficient to produce a function \(f_j(T)\) compensating the temperature error of one sensor, which will then make it possible to compensate all the sensors by multiplying (dividing) the value of the compensating signal by an appropriate proportionality coefficient.

4.4. The essence of incomplete temperature compensation

We shall now consider the value of the error (26) of the transducer under the assumption of incomplete compensation. By incomplete compensation will be understood a limitation of the number of compensation temperatures in comparison with the values obtained from relations (38) or (42).

$$ m \cdot n < s \qquad (43) $$

In the case \(s = 1\) the temperature error is reduced identically to zero by a compensation circuit with the parameters \(m = 1\), \(n = 1\).

4.5. Summary

The proposed method of compensation of the temperature error of the lower and upper range values of the measuring range of a pressure transducer consists in summing the value of the parameter under consideration with the value of the signal of the compensating-signal generator, so that at n temperatures the value of this parameter becomes equal to its value at the reference temperature. The number n of compensation temperatures depends on the value of the sensor error and on the value of the permissible transducer error.

If the number n of compensation temperatures is equal to the degree of the polynomial describing the temperature error of the given parameter of the sensor characteristic, and the signals of the compensating generators are linearly dependent on temperature, then the compensation procedure presented leads unambiguously to the identical vanishing of the transducer error over the whole operating temperature range under consideration, irrespective of the choice of the values of the compensation temperatures.

If a certain number of properties of the temperature-error characteristic of the sensor is known in advance, this fact can be used to reduce the number of temperatures necessary for reducing the transducer error identically to zero.

If complete compensation of the error is not required, it is proposed to use the method with a number n of compensation temperatures smaller by one than the degree of the polynomial describing the temperature error of the sensor, with the signals of the compensating generators linearly dependent on temperature.

The compensation procedure for a given parameter is characterised by unidirectional independence, which means that establishing the compensation conditions for this parameter at the current compensation temperature does not change the conditions at the previous compensation temperatures. Thanks to this, the necessity is eliminated of iterative determination, which requires several temperature cycles, or of calculation (most often by means of a digital computer) of the values of the components of the compensation circuit necessary for compensating the temperature error of the parameter of the transducer characteristic

<!-- scan p073 | printed page 70 | type: text -->

under consideration.

Comparing the method presented with other known methods [1, 32–76], it must be stated that only the method presented in this thesis makes possible the identical reduction of the temperature error of the transducer to zero together with unidirectional independence of the establishment of the compensation conditions.

In the method presented, the change of the temperature characteristic of the compensating signal is effected by changing proportionality coefficients that are independent of temperature. In practical application the compensation method may consist in appropriately setting potentiometric dividers of the compensating signals. Since on such dividers only a signal of the order of the temperature errors of the sensor would then be present, that is, a small percentage of the full-scale signal, the metrological requirements imposed on these components could be relaxed.

The use of the potentiometric dividers mentioned would eliminate the necessity of stocking a large number of values of compensating components (which may potentially be used) or of manufacturing them after obtaining information on the sensor errors.

The method presented can be applied in measuring transducers of all types for non-electrical quantities satisfying the conditions listed in Section 4.1.
